What It Takes: Quota Benchmarks by Class

Each card shows the best-5 OQS total needed for a quota place, four ways:

  • Lowest qualifier 22/23 and 24/25: the weakest athlete who actually won a place under the ICF proposal, with their five counting placings and the first athlete who missed out.
  • Simulated typical (P50) and safe (P80): each cycle is replayed thousands of times with its races drawn at random (with replacement), and the full allocation is re-run each time. A total at the P80 level would have won a place in 80% of simulated cycles.
  • Ways to get there: combinations of five placings that reach the total even if every result is at the bottom of its band (e.g. every "Top 5" scored as 5th). The most consistent routes are listed first.

Assumptions

  • ICF proposal quotas (Kayak 6, Canoe 10, Kayak Cross 3), one place per nation, best 5 results counted. Allocation runs Kayak → Kayak Cross → Canoe, and an athlete who already holds a place is skipped.
  • Benchmarks assume the athlete is their nation's top-ranked athlete. For athletes from strong nations, the real bar is often beating a team-mate, not the cut-off.
  • Only two cycles of results exist. The simulation shows how much the cut-off moves with the races drawn, but it cannot predict a shift in the overall standard of the field.
  • Continental, host-nation and universality places are not modelled.

The "Top X" columns show how many of each athlete's counting results (up to 5) fall in each rank band; bands are independent (each result counts in one column only). K1/C1: Top 3 = ranks 1–3, Top 6 = 4–6, Top 12 = 7–12, Top 20 = 13–20. Cross: Top 4 = 1–4, Top 8 = 5–8, Top 12 = 9–12, Top 16 = 13–16. Results outside these bands count in no column, so the Top X columns need not add up to 5.

The "Top N UN" column shows how many of the athlete's counting races (the races that make up their OQS points, up to 5) they finished inside the top N unique nations as the first athlete from their own nation. Because a quota place belongs to a nation rather than an athlete, only a nation's leading finisher in a race is credited: if a team-mate finishes ahead of them, that nation is already counted and the athlete scores nothing for that race, however high they placed. So this column can be lower than the rank bands suggest — an athlete who finishes 3rd behind a winning team-mate is credited in "Top 3" but not here. N = 6 for Kayak, 10 for Canoe, 3 for Kayak Cross. Where athletes tie on the boundary rank, all tied nations are credited.

Athletes are ranked by total OQS points, then by average finishing rank across their counting races. The average is shown rounded to the nearest whole number but ranked on its exact value, so two athletes can display the same average and still be separated. Results with no rank or no points recorded (DNS/DNF) are excluded.
